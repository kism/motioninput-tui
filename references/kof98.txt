 1.  HOW TO PLAY

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 FAQ NOTATION
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Here's a quick representation of the game's controls:

 ub   u   uf      Jump Backward         Jump Up      Jump Forward
   \  |  /
 b -- n -- f      Retreat/Block         Neutral      Approach
   /  |  \
 db   d   df      Defensive Crouch      Crouch       Offensive Crouch

 The buttons are layed out like so:


    B  C  D                     Weak Kick    Strong Punch    Strong Kick
 A                Weak Punch


 Notation used in this FAQ includes:

 P      - Press A / C.
 K      - Press C / D.
 qcf    - Press d,df,f  (reverse is qcb or d,db,b)
 hcf    - Press b,db,d,df,f  (reverse is hcb or f,df,d,db,b)
 (air)  - the move can be performed while you're jumping.
 dir.   - any of the eight directions can be used.
 Charge - Hold in the listed direction for two seconds, then push the
          joystick in the second direction.
 From   - Some moves can be followed with another move.  This will be
          followed by the move you can use to interrupt what your
          character is currently doing.
 =#     - =1 means that the move is only cancellable on the first hit.
          =2 means that it's only cancellable on the second hit.
          =e means that it's cancellable on either hit.


 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USING ADVANCED OR EXTRA MODE
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 KoF '98, like it's predecessor, has two basic modes of gameplay.  They
 are explained in greater detail below:

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 IN ADVANCED MODE
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Block / Mid-Air Block          Hold b / db  (air)
 Backstep                       Tap b,b
 Dash (Running)                 Tap f,f  (hold f to run)
 Small Jump                     Tap ub~uf
 Middle Jump                    Press ub~uf
 Big Jump                       Tap d,ub / tap d,uf / dash, then jump
 Emergency Evade (Forward)      A + B
 Emergency Evade (Backward)     b + A + B
 Down Evade                     Press A + B as you land
 Throw Escape                   b / f + P / K as you're thrown
 Guard Cancel Emergency Evade   Block, then A + B
 Guard Cancel Strike Attack     Block, then C + D
 Strike Attack                  Press C + D  (air)
 Activate Power Gauge           Press A + B + C  (1 point)
 Cover Attack                   Tap A + B + C rapidly when dizzied
 Taunt                          Press Start

 - When you block against an attack, you take no damage from normal
   moves and command attacks, but you do take reduced damage from
   specials and (S)DMs.  Throws and certain other moves may be
   unblockable, though.

 - You're semi-invincible during either Emergency Evade, although you
   can still be hit by command throws (throws that are specific to each
   character and not the b / f + C / D variety).

 - Using the Down Evade makes your character leap back just as he or she
   hits the ground, and land on their feet, instead of crashing to the
   floor.  This move can be used after knockdown attacks, or other
   standard knockdown moves like most character's crouching D attack.

 - The GC Emergency Evade works just like a backwards Emergency Evade,
   except that you can do it while blocking against an attack.  Ditto
   with the GC Strike Attack.  In order to use these moves, you must
   have activated your Power Gauge by pressing A + B + C.  However,
   using these moves does not detract from the time bar, so you can use
   them as often as you want until your power runs out normally.

 - Someone hit by a Strike Attack is knocked down to the floor.

 - The Cover Attack has several prerequisites that must be met in
   order fo it to work.  First, your life must be at 50% or less.
   Second, you must be dizzy, _or_ be stuck in a grab or repeated
   attack, such as Choi's Zujou Sashi (his C-button throw), or Chang's
   Dai Hakai Nage.  Thirdly, you must have an undefeated teammate
   standing on-screen and nearby.  Finally, that teammate must have a
   neutral or friendly relationship towards you.  Also note that if
   your life is lower than your opponent's, you have a higher chance
   of being helped by your teammates.  On a personal note, I haven't
   been able to get this to work yet, so I'm unsure if it's just hard
   to pull off, or if the command I have listed is wrong.

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE POWER GAUGE IN ADVANCED MODE
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  You can fill up your Power Gauge by:

    1.  Performing special moves (whether or not they hit).
    2.  Damaging your opponent with anything aside from a DM/SDM.
    3.  Getting damaged by your opponent.

  When it's full, it then empties, but you gain 1 green POW point.
  You can hold:

    1.  Three points when playing with your first character.
    2.  Four points when using your second character.
    3.  Five points when you're controlling your last team member.

  Accumulated POW points can be expended to:

    1.  Use the 'Activate Power Gauge' move (press A + B + C).
    2.  Use a DM (Desperation Move).

  When you activate your Power Gauge, it turns into a timer bar that
  slowly empties.  While the timer bar is on-screen:

    1.  You can use the GC Emergency Evade or GC Strike Attack.  They
        can be used repeatedly without draining any power from the timer
        bar or Power Gauge.
    2.  You can perform a SDM (Super Desperation Move).  This is done
        with the same command as a DM and costs an additional POW point
        (just like when using a DM), but since your Power Gauge has been
        activated, you'll perform the SDM version instead.
    3.  Your body will flash, and any attack you make does 25% more
        damage.

  You can lose Power Gauge energy, POW points, or your activated Power
  Gauge under these conditions:

    1.  Activating your Power Gauge or performing a DM or SDM all cost
        one point.
    2.  Between rounds, POW points are carried over, but Power Gauge
        energy that has been accumulated is not (the gauge empties.
        Even if it is almost full, you still won't gain a point).
    3.  If your Power Gauge has been activated, it returns to normal
        between rounds.
    4.  If you perform an SDM, your Power Gauge returns to normal
        whether or not the move hit or not.
    5.  If you wait too long after activating your Power Gauge, then
        the timer bar empties and the Power Gauge goes back to normal.


 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE POWER GAUGE IN EXTRA MODE
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  You can fill up your Power Gauge by:

    1.  Charging up your Power Gauge  (hold down A + B + C).
    2.  Getting damaged by your opponent.

  The Power Gauge shrinks every time you lose, making it easier to
  fill it up.  It shrinks to:

    1.  75% of it's normal size when playing with your second character.
    2.  50% of it's normal size when using your final character.

  When your Power Gauge is filled, it says 'MAXIMUM' and slowly begins
  to empty.  While the Power Gauge is emptying:

    1.  You can use the GC Emergency Evade or GC Strike Attack.  Using
        either one of them will completely empty the Power Gauge.
    2.  You can perform a DM (Desperation Move).
    3.  Your body will flash, and any attack you make does 50% more
        damage.

  If you take enough damage (if your life is at 20% or less), your
  Life Gauge begins flashing red.  When this happens:

    1.  You can perform DMs repeatedly.
    2.  You can use the GC Emergency Evade or GC Strike Attack.  They
        can be used repeatedly without draining any power from the
        Power Gauge.
    3.  If your Power Gauge was maxed out and is now emptying, then
        you can use SDMs instead of DMs.  Doing so will not affect
        your life, but it will empty your Power Bar completely.

  Note that if your life is raised beyond 20%  (this may happen if
  you win the round, since you gain back some life, or if you're
  using Heidern's restorative "Bringer" moes), then your Life Gauge
  stops flashing and you lose all benefits of having a low Life Gauge.

  You can lose Power Gauge energy under these conditions:

    1.  Performing a DM or SDM when the Power Gauge is full will empty
        it.
    2.  Between rounds, all accumulated Power Gauge energy is lost,
        even if it was almost full.
    3.  If your Power Gauge had been maxed out and is now emptying,
        it returns to normal between rounds.
    4.  If you perform an SDM, your Power Gauge returns to normal
        whether or not the move hit or not.
    5.  If you wait too long after maxing out your Power Gauge, then
        it empties and the Power Gauge goes back to normal.


 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USING COMMAND ATTACKS
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 There are three rules to keep in mind when using a command attack:

  - Some command attacks are overhead moves, most can be comboed into,
    and most be cancelled into a special move.
  - _But_, if you combo into a command attack, it loses any special
    properties it had (it won't work as an overhead or knockdown
    attack, for example).
  - However, for some command attacks, you _have_ to combo into
    them first if you want to then cancel them into a special move.

 The general idea is that, if a command attack has a special feature
 (overheads or knockdowns), it must be comboed into if you want to
 cancel from it.  Otherwise, you should be able to cancel it into a
 special move just fine (but like I said earlier, not all command
 attacks can be cancelled from--see each characters's move list below
 for more information).

 Let's use Saishu for an example (and not just because he's one of my
 favorite characters):

  - Saishu's Gou Tsui (f + A) is an overhead attack.  Normally, he
    _cannot_ cancel it into a special move (such as a Yami Barai).

  - However, if he uses a close standing C, then combos that into the
    Gou Tsui, he can then cancel the Gou Tsuchi into the Yami Barai.
    This 3-hit combo looks like this:

                     Crouching C, f + A, qcf + P

  - Saishu's other command attack is the Kubu Tsuchi (f + B).  This
    move doesn't have any special properties.  However, it can be
    comboed from.  For instance, Saishu could use the Kubu Tsuchi, then
    the Oniyaki, like this:

                     When close, f + B, f,d,df + P

  - If he wanted to he could even combo into the Kubu Tsuchi, like so:

                     Standing C, f + B, f,d,df + P

 Remember that these rules apply to every command attack.  Even in rare
 cases like Leona's Strike Arch (which is both an overhead and a
 knockdown attack), if you proceeded it with a standing C, then inputted
 f + B for the Strike Arch, it would not knock down and a crouching
 blocking opponent would be able to guard against it.


 ========================================================================
 2.  CHARACTER MOVELISTS
 ========================================================================

 Each character movelist is composed of several parts: at the top is
 the character's name and the team they're in, followed by a short bio
 sheet, then their win quote and a short list of moves.  After that comes
 a longer, more detailed list of moves.  Both lists are written in this
 order: normal throws, command attacks, special moves, and (S)DMs.  After
 the detailed moveslist is a chart of cancelable attacks, although some
 characters may also have a 'notes' section or a flowchart of their
 special move chains.

 As for specifics, the number of hits for each move was determined using
 Chang as my opponent (since he's so big, you have a better chance of
 discovering what the total number of hits for each move actually is).

 All translations were done by me, with help from others in certain
 casesm, so there may be some mistakes, as I don't know much Japanese.
 To make matters even worse, the move names for the Real Orochi Team
 were originally written, or at least sounded out, in Russian (just like
 Goenitz's moves back in '96).  While I've been able to find kana so as
 to properly spell the names, I can't figure out what the move names
 mean, since I don't have the kanji.  If anyone has the specific kanji
 for their move names, I'd love to hear from ya.

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 PLAY AS THE HIDDEN CHARACTERS
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 To play as a hidden character, just highlight their counterpart, hold
 Start, then press any button.  The characters with hidden counterparts
 include: Kyo, the Fatal Fury Team, the Art of Fighting Team, Mai
 Shiranui, Billy Kane, the Orochi Team, and Rugal Bernstein (you
 can only select Omega Rugal in the home versions, though).


 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 USING THE ROULETTE
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 To pick a random character, go to the roulette (the box with a question
 mark in it between Rugal and Shingo).  You can select a random character
 for  one, two, or all of your team's characters.  Afterwards, you'll get
 a new character to fight with in each battle.


 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 FIGHT SHINGO YABUKI
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 To fight Shingo Yabuki alone, you have to fulfill one of these
 requirements:

  - Prior to the fourth match, you have to have over 200,000 points.
    If you do this, Shingo challenges you on your 4th match.  If not,
    read below:

  - Prior to the fifth match, you have to have fulfilled the first
    condition (over 200,000 points), as well as having gotten a
    perfect victory in the previous rounds (i.e. one character is
    never hit).  If you do this, Shingo challenges you on the 5th
    match.  If not, read below:

  - Prior to the sixth match, make sure that you've fulfilled the
    previous requirements (over 200,000 points and one perfect victory),
    and make sure that none of your fellow members die (get a straight
    win) during the previous match.  If you do this, Shingo challenges
    you on the sixth match.

 To be honest, though, I always end up fighting Shingo no matter what I

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 ATTITUDE INFORMATION
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 Knowing how your characters are feeling is important because it
 determines the chance of them helping you when you attempt a Cover
 Attack.  In addition, if you're using Advanced Mode, it affects the
 number of POW points you have.  I haven't noticed any effect that it
 has if you're using Extra Mode.  The faces and their meanings are
 as follows:

  Yellow, happy-looking face:
   - You're more likely to be helped / Advanced users gain 1 point.

  Gray, bored face:
   - You might not be helped / No change in points for Advanced users.

  Angry, red face:
   - You'll never be helped / Advanced users lose all points.

 Unlike KoF '97, a character's attitude does not change depending on
 his or her partners--it just represents that character's attitude
 in general.  In the arcade version, each person's attitude changed
 daily, but in the home version, each person always has a specific
 attitude, listed below:

 Happy characters:  Billy, Chang Koehan, Heavy D!, Iori, King, Saishu,
                    Shingo, Sie Kensou, Takuma, Terry Bogard, Vice.

 Angry characters:  Choi, Chris, Heidern, Joe Higashi, Kim Kaphwan,
                    Leona, Lucky, Mature, Rugal, Ryo Sakazaki.

 Bored characters:  Everyone else.

 To see the attitude of your characters, select your team.  Then, when
 asked to arrange who fights in what order, hold Start and the faces
 will be displayed.  To understand how the faces work, I'll make up a
 mock example of a certain character line-up:

      - Goro is going first and has a bored face.
      - Leona is going second and has an angry face.
      - Shingo is going third and has a happy face.

 In this setup, Goro will never be helped by Leona, since Leona has an
 angry face and so will never take part in a Cover Attack.  Let's say
 Goro loses and Leona is now fighting.  If he attempts a Cover Attack,
 Shingo will always try to help her since Shingo has a happy face (he
 likes everybody, even psychos like Leona).  If Leona loses and Shingo
 is fighting, he cannot use a Cover Attack.  This is simply because
 there are now no more undefeated teammates left.

 On the other hand, if, for example, Shingo went first and Goro went
 second, then Shingo would sometimes get help from Goro, and sometimes
 not.  This is because Goro has a neutral face and so he doesn't really
 feel like helping Shingo all the time.

 Now, let's use the same characters, but this time we'll look at what
 happens to your DM points if you're in Advanced Mode.

      - Goro is going first and has a bored face.
      - Leona is going second and has an angry face.
      - Shingo is going third and has a happy face.

 Goro amasses enough power during the round to earn 2 POW points, but
 he's defeated before he can use them.  Leona, who's up next, will
 have those 2 POW points, since Goro is netural and there is no change
 in points.  However, if Leona loses with 2 POW points still remaining,
 then Shingo will start off with nothing, because Leona is angry and
 is probably hoping Shingo will lose.

 However, let's say Shingo was the second character and Leona was
 third.  Shingo would still have those two points that he earned from
 Goro.  If he's defeated in battle, then Leona will start off with
 three points, since Shingo's a happy fellow and wants to help her
 out.

 Got it?  Yep, I know it's confusing!  You can check out Section 1
 (FAQ Notation) for more information on using Cover Attacks.

 RYO SAKAZAKI / TAKUMA SAKAZAKI / YURI SAKAZAKI

 BLUE MARY VS. TERRY BOGARD

 CHIN GENTSAI VS. ATHENA ASAMIYA

 CHIZURU KAGURA VS. IORI YAGAMI / KYO KUSANAGI
  - Chizuru motions her hand in front of her in a circle motion.  She

 CHIZURU KAGURA VS. CHRIS / MATURE / RUGAL / RYUJI / SHERMIE / VICE /
                    YASHIRO NANAKASE

    from the floor, and shakes it in front of her.  She then puts it
    back to the floor.

 CHRIS VS. SHERMIE
 - Chris rises his hand and says "Shermie!", and Shermie waves towards
   Chris, saying "Hi!"

 CHRIS VS. YASHIRO NANAKASE

 CLARK STEEL VS. HEIDERN / LEONA

 HEAVY D! VS. BRIAN BATTLER / LUCKY GLAUBER

 ------------------------------------------------------------------------
 TRANSLATION NOTES
 ------------------------------------------------------------------------

 The Japanese move names were mainly translated by myself, so there are
 probably a few mistakes or misinterpretations.  Feel free to drop me
 a line and let me know what I slipped up on should you spot anything.

 Regarding the move names; SNK originally gave generic names to some of
 the new moves in KoF '98, then went back later and came up with the
 real, "offical" names.  In the KoF '98 booklet that was being sold with
 the home version of the Japanese Neo*Geo cart, the temporary move names
 were simply crossed out, and the real ones written nearby.  However,
 the handwriting is near impossible for me to make out (plus I was
 reading it from a scanned photo, since I don't own the book nor the
 Neo*Geo home version myself).  As a result, I don't have the official
 name for Shingo's "Hiji Otoshi", and I can't be entirely sure of some
 of the other move names (I'm pretty sure Omega Rugal's "Gravity Smash"
 is wrong as well).  If I don't have the official or temporary name for
 a move, it's written in parentheses, in lowercase.

 In other cases where I'm unsure of a translation, a question mark
 appears in the brackets.  This is mainly due to the fact that a kanji
 character written in kana could mean just about anything--all you
 know is it's spelling, and you have to make educated guesses.  (Then
 again, in the case of the Orochi Team, I'd rather have all the moves
 in hiragana than in Russian, as they were originally written!)

 Also, some moves have more than one name (for Kyo and Yuri, in
 particular).  SNK just gave the same move two different names, they're
 not for the different button strengths, as far as I know.  Go figure.

 Finally, although his name is spelled 'Saisyu' in the game, it's
 spelled here as "Saishu".  It's not incorrect, just a different (and
 a little more pronouncable) spelling.
